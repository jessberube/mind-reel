    About this app

    Mind Reel is a personal tool for practicing rapid image cycling. The technique comes from William Bengston, who named it Image Cycling®. To learn the method from the source, visit bengstonresearch.com.

    Who was William Bengston?

    William “Bill” Bengston (1950–2025) was a sociologist from the United States. He taught research methods and statistics at St. Joseph’s College in New York, now St. Joseph’s University, for about 40 years. He also led the Society for Scientific Exploration from 2010 to 2022. He studied hands-on healing for more than four decades and called himself a skeptic who wanted to test it. He died on April 16, 2025, at age 75.

    How the practice works
    1. List 20 or more things you want for yourself. Be specific. Pick a real place you want to see, not a feeling like “be happy.”
    2. Give each goal one clear picture that shows it already done.
    3. Learn the list in order until each picture pops up right away.
    4. Run through the pictures in your mind. Start at about one per second. Then go faster, until the whole list goes by in about a second. As you speed up, let go of how each one makes you feel.
    5. Keep it easy and do a little every day. When you get something, or stop wanting it, swap in a new goal.

    Once this feels natural, some people try hypercycling. They put all the pictures on something spinning in their mind, like a wheel or a clock.

    Why go so fast?

    Speed gives your busy, thinking mind a job, so it stays out of the way. Bengston compared it to a guard dog. Give the dog something to chew on, and it leaves you alone.

    • Goals for you. The list is about what you want, not what you want for other people. Giving that part of you what it wants keeps it quiet.
    • Speed. Going fast leaves no time to judge, hope, or linger on any one picture.
    • Let go of results. Pushing hard for an outcome is the kind of effort this practice avoids. Stay relaxed. A little bored is fine.
